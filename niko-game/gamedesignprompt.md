# Niko the Ninja That Could! — Game Design Prompt

> A ready-to-use **master prompt** for pitching, prototyping, or generating a
> long-form, story-driven adventure game set in the Niko universe — in the
> spirit of *Fable*, *Ni no Kuni*, *Spiritfarer*, and *The Legend of Zelda*,
> but built for children and families.
>
> Use the [Master Prompt](#master-prompt) verbatim (paste it into a design AI,
> a studio brief, or an engine's scenario generator). The sections after it are
> the design pillars the prompt is distilled from — expand or trim as needed.
> All character and art rules defer to [`../series-bible.md`](../series-bible.md).

## Master Prompt

> **Copy everything in this block.**

```
You are designing "Niko the Ninja That Could!", a long-form, single-player,
story-driven adventure game for children and families (rated E / PEGI 3),
in the spirit of Fable, Ni no Kuni, and Spiritfarer.

CORE FANTASY
The player IS Niko Madziwa, a small, clumsy, endlessly determined ninja growing
up in the hidden village of Kageyama — a place where "a strong ninja helps
others rise." You do not win by fighting. You win by helping, persevering,
solving, and caring. Every problem has a clever, kind, or creative solution
instead of a violent one.

THE HOOK: NIKO AGES
This is a life-sim spine wrapped around an adventure game. Niko ages from a
toddler to a 13-year-old leader across four life stages, and the player grows
with him. Ability, world size, and story stakes all scale up as he ages:
  - Age 0-4  (Origins):   family, first lessons, learning to move & try.
  - Age 5-7  (Academy):   ninja school, first friends, first adventures.
  - Age 8-10 (Wider World): new villages, dragons, mysteries, festivals.
  - Age 11-13 (Leadership): mentoring younger ninjas, bigger challenges.
Choices, friendships, and skills carry forward between stages. The player should
feel they raised this ninja.

THE "COULD" SYSTEM (progression = perseverance, not power)
Niko's catchphrase is "Maybe I can't do it yet..." — emphasis on YET. Every
skill starts as a wobbly, funny failure and is mastered through practice, not
grinding for XP. Attempting hard things and failing gently still rewards the
player (encouragement, hints, a sister's cheer). The core loop is:
TRY -> stumble -> get a kind nudge -> TRY AGAIN -> master it -> help someone
else who is now where you were.

THE KINDNESS METER (morality, reimagined)
Instead of good-vs-evil, track how much Niko "helps others rise." Kind,
patient, creative, and brave acts raise the village's warmth toward Niko and
unlock cooperative content, new friends, and village growth. There is no evil
path and no punishment for failure — only richer rewards for kindness. The
world visibly blossoms (more lanterns, flowers, rebuilt bridges, happier NPCs)
as the meter grows.

PILLARS OF PLAY (non-combat verbs)
  - MOVEMENT & NINJA SKILLS: climbing, balancing, stealth (hide-and-seek, not
    assassination), rooftop traversal, grappling, gentle "ghost" dodging.
  - PUZZLES & INVENTION: Niko is creative — combine tools, build contraptions
    (echoing Daichi's "big things are built one piece at a time").
  - CARE & COMMUNITY: quests are chores, rescues, festivals, and helping
    neighbours; cooking/healing minigames from Hana; growing the village.
  - COMPANION MISCHIEF: Biscuit the Raccoon can be sent to fetch, distract, and
    (comically) cause the very problems Niko must then solve — the running gag
    is a real mechanic.

WORLD
Kageyama: a hidden village between mountains, forests, waterfalls, and
cherry-blossom valleys. A cosy hub that physically grows and heals as the
player helps. Beyond it, an expanding overworld of new villages, a dragon's
mountain, festival grounds, and gentle mysteries unlocked by life stage.

CAST (playable / companions / mentors)
  - Niko (player): curious, determined, kind, clumsy, creative. Never gives up.
  - Akiko (12, big sister): disciplined mentor & role model; teaches core
    skills; catchphrase "Let's try one more time."
  - Hana (mother): baker & herbal healer; cooking/healing systems.
  - Daichi (father): carpenter & builder; crafting/building systems.
  - Sensei Willow: mysterious old master; unlocks skill trees, hides biscuits,
    says "Interesting..."; the quest-giver of the Academy stage onward.
  - Biscuit the Raccoon: mischievous companion; puzzle/comedy mechanic.

STORY STRUCTURE
Adapt the seven canonical books as the game's main "chapters," each anchored to
its theme and life stage:
  1. Niko is Born - Family (tutorial: movement, the village, meeting the cast)
  2. Niko Takes His First Steps - Trying (the TRY-AGAIN loop is taught here)
  3. Niko's First Day at Ninja School - Courage (Academy hub, skill trees open)
  4. Niko and the Midnight Obstacle Course - Everyone has different strengths
     (party/friend co-solve; celebrate diverse abilities)
  5. Niko and the Dragon's Secret - Understanding others (befriend, don't fight)
  6. Niko vs The Shadow Sneezes - Creativity (an invention/puzzle chapter)
  7. Niko and the Lost Kitten Rescue - Helping others (open-ended rescue sandbox)
Between chapters, free-roam side content lets Niko age, practise, and help.

TONE & THEMES
Warm, funny, encouraging, gently emotional. Themes: perseverance, family,
kindness, courage, creativity, and that everyone has different strengths. Fail
states are soft and encouraging. No death, no realistic violence, no horror.

ART & AUDIO DIRECTION (LOCKED — see the Visual Style Bible)
Storybook quality with Studio Ghibli warmth and Bluey character appeal: bright
colours, soft lighting, cinematic compositions, landscape framing. Niko ALWAYS
has black hair and dark brown eyes, a navy outfit, and a red sash — never blonde
hair, never blue eyes. No hyper-realism, no dark/horror aesthetics. Audio:
gentle orchestral + Japanese-inspired instrumentation, cosy ambient village
sound, warm voice-over suited to read-aloud ages.

WHAT TO PRODUCE
When responding to this prompt, deliver: (1) a one-paragraph elevator pitch,
(2) the core gameplay loop, (3) the four-stage progression design with sample
skills per stage, (4) the Kindness Meter rules and rewards, (5) a chapter
breakdown mapping the seven books to playable content, (6) 5-8 sample side
quests, (7) an art & audio brief, and (8) a target platform & scope
recommendation. Keep everything age-appropriate, non-violent, and consistent
with the cast and Visual Style Bible above.
```

## Design pillars (the reasoning behind the prompt)

### 1. Why Fable-style fits Niko
Fable's signature is *a hero who visibly ages and whose choices reshape the
world*. Niko already ships with an **aging timeline** and a **moral compass**
("a strong ninja helps others rise"). We keep Fable's life-arc and
consequence-driven world, but swap combat morality for **kindness and
perseverance** — a wholesome reframe that suits ages 3–10 and their parents.

### 2. Genre & references
- **Fable** — aging hero, reputation shapes the world.
- **Ni no Kuni** — Ghibli-warm storybook JRPG feel.
- **Spiritfarer** — cosy management, care as a core verb, gentle emotion.
- **Zelda (BotW/TotK "shrine" logic)** — traversal + inventive puzzles.
- **Bluey / A Short Hike** — tone, humour, character appeal, bite-sized joy.

### 3. Progression without violence
| Life stage | Niko's age | Sample unlockable skills |
|-----------|-----------|--------------------------|
| Origins | 0–4 | Crawl → walk → run; first jump; "brave breath" (calm nerves) |
| Academy | 5–7 | Wall-cling, balance beam, silent-step (hide & seek), grapple hook |
| Wider World | 8–10 | Rooftop dash, glider (blossom-leaf), animal-speak, tool-crafting |
| Leadership | 11–13 | Team commands, mentoring moves, big-contraption building |

Skills are mastered by **practising through failure**, never by grinding XP off
enemies. Sensei Willow's skill trees gate new abilities behind story, not stats.

### 4. The Kindness Meter (morality system)
- Raised by: helping neighbours, patience, creative solutions, encouraging other
  young ninjas, finishing festivals, caring for animals.
- **No evil path.** The only variable is *how much the village flourishes*.
- Visible feedback: the world blossoms — more lanterns at night, repaired
  bridges, planted gardens, happier NPC routines, a growing Kageyama hub.
- Rewards: new companions, cosmetic sashes, recipes/blueprints, village upgrades,
  and story cutscenes — never power that trivialises challenge.

### 5. Core loop (one screen)
```
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  Explore Kageyama & beyond                       │
   │        │                                         │
   │        ▼                                         │
   │  Meet someone who needs help / hit a challenge   │
   │        │                                         │
   │        ▼                                         │
   │  TRY  →  stumble (soft, funny fail)              │
   │        │        ▲                                │
   │        ▼        │ kind nudge from family/mentor  │
   │  TRY AGAIN  ─────┘                               │
   │        │                                         │
   │        ▼                                         │
   │  Master it  →  Kindness Meter ↑  →  village grows│
   │        │                                         │
   │        ▼                                         │
   │  Now help someone who is where you just were     │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 6. Sample side quests
1. **Biscuit's Custard Caper** — Biscuit steals the festival biscuits; track the
   crumb trail across rooftops before the celebration (comedy + traversal).
2. **The Wobbly Bridge** — gather wood with Daichi and rebuild a bridge "one
   piece at a time" so an elder can cross (crafting + patience).
3. **Lantern Night** — light every lantern in the village before dusk to welcome
   a new baby (timed traversal + kindness payoff, echoes Book 1).
4. **Hana's Poultice** — forage herbs, complete the healing minigame to soothe a
   sneezing dragon-cub (cooking/healing + Understanding-others theme).
5. **Akiko's One More Time** — a rhythm/practice challenge on the training beam;
   fail as many times as you like — completion celebrates persistence, not speed.
6. **The Different-Strengths Relay** — recruit friends whose abilities (climbing,
   swimming, throwing) each clear one leg of a course no one can finish alone.
7. **Lost Kitten Rescue** — an open sandbox: multiple valid ways to coax a scared
   kitten down from the great cherry tree (Book 7 in miniature).

### 7. Scope recommendation
- **Vertical slice first:** Book 1 + Book 3 content (tutorial village + Academy
  hub) proves the age-up transition and the TRY-AGAIN loop.
- **Platforms:** Nintendo Switch + PC as lead (family/handheld fit), with a
  tablet-friendly control scheme; controller and simple touch both supported.
- **Engine:** Unity or Godot for a small team; stylised toon shading over
  hyper-real assets keeps it on-brand and performant.
- **Session shape:** short, self-contained "help someone" loops (5–15 min) so
  young players get frequent, complete wins — with a longer chapter spine for
  those who want it.

## Notes for whoever builds this
- Everything visual defers to the **Visual Style Bible** in
  [`../series-bible.md`](../series-bible.md). Niko's black hair / dark brown eyes,
  navy outfit, and red sash are **non-negotiable** even as he ages.
- Keep fail states soft: no "Game Over," only "Maybe not yet — let's try one
  more time."
- The franchise's emotional promise — *effort and kindness matter more than
  being the best* — must survive every design trade-off.
